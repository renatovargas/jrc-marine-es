STECF Fleet Segment Data

From one large workbook to two analysis-ready datasets

Why this page exists

The fish provisioning accounts are built from a single Excel workbook published by the Scientific, Technical and Economic Committee for Fisheries (STECF). That workbook is 285 MB, which is too large to keep under version control and slow to open on most machines. This page records what the workbook contains, how we extracted the parts we need, and what the extraction is safe to be used for.

The conversion is done once by data/stecf/stecf_to_rds.R. Its two outputs are committed to the repository; the workbook itself is listed in .gitignore and stays on local machines only. Anyone who needs to regenerate the RDS files can download the workbook again and rerun the script.

The source workbook

The file is STECF_25 07_EU Fleet Economic and Transversal data_fleet segment level.xlsx, released under the 2025 Fleet Economic data call. It holds twelve sheets:

FS_effort by sub-region Effort by fleet segment and FAO sub-region

FS_landings FAO 2018-2023 Landings by species and sub-region, 2018 to 2024
pivot_landings Excel pivot of the above
FS_landings FAO 2013-2017 Landings by species and sub-region
FS_landings FAO 2008-2012 Landings by species and sub-region
additional info Country codes, gear codes, inflation and interest rates
codes_species FAO ASFIS code, scientific name, common name
codes__regions_sub-regions Sub-region code, FAO area, supra-region

We extract four of them: the three landings sheets and FS data. The pivot sheets are Excel artefacts of the same numbers. The effort sheets are not used by the fish provisioning method. The three code sheets duplicate lookups we already curate in data/lookup, so we leave them where they are for now.

ImportantThe sheet names understate their content

FS_landings FAO 2018-2023 contains 2024 as well, and FS data runs to 2024. The output files are therefore named for the years they actually hold, 2008 to 2024, rather than for the sheet labels.

What the script does

data/stecf/stecf_to_rds.R reads the four sheets, binds the three landings sheets into one table, and writes two RDS files into data/stecf/. Run it from the project root:

Rscript data/stecf/stecf_to_rds.R

It takes about 35 seconds and turns 285 MB of Excel into 18 MB of RDS.

Three design decisions are worth recording.

Column types are declared, not guessed. Left to itself, readxl infers cluster_name as logical in the 2018-2023 sheet, where that column happens to be empty, and as text in the other two; binding the three together would then fail. The script declares all 24 types by name and compares them against the actual header before reading. A future data call that renames or reorders a column stops the script with an explicit message rather than producing a quietly wrong table.

The script converts, it does not clean. No rows are dropped and no values are recoded. Rows where value is missing are kept as they appear in the workbook, because a missing value is meaningful: it usually indicates that a Member State did not report that variable, or that the figure was suppressed for confidentiality. Deciding what to do with them belongs to the analysis, not to the format conversion.

Each row records the sheet it came from. A source_sheet column is added so that any question about provenance can be answered from the dataset itself.

Are they the same data?

They are different tables that overlap in one variable. FS data reports the gross value of landings for a fleet segment as totlandginc; the landings sheets report the same economic concept as totvallandg, broken down by species and sub-region. The two agree closely without being identical.

value_by_year <- bind_rows(
  fs_data |>
    filter(variable_code == "totlandginc") |>
    summarise(euro = sum(value, na.rm = TRUE), .by = year) |>
    mutate(source = "FS data"),
  fs_landings |>
    filter(variable_code == "totvallandg") |>
    summarise(euro = sum(value, na.rm = TRUE), .by = year) |>
    mutate(source = "FS landings")
) |>
  pivot_wider(names_from = source, values_from = euro) |>
  mutate(difference_pct = round(100 * (`FS landings` - `FS data`) / `FS data`, 1)) |>
  arrange(year)

From 2009 to 2023 the two series differ by less than three percent, so the landings sheets are effectively a disaggregation of the same reporting. They are not an exact one, because the two templates are submitted separately by Member States and because confidentiality suppression and clustering apply differently at the two levels of detail. The 2008 and 2024 gaps are larger for the reasons given below.

The practical consequence for the fish provisioning method is a clean division of labour. Live weight in kilograms exists only in the landings sheets, so the biophysical account has to come from there. Income and expenditure exist only in FS data, so the net economic result has to come from there. That is why the routine reads both.

What the NOTES sheet says

The workbook opens with a page of caveats. These are the ones that bear on our use of it.

Fleet segments and their names. A fleet segment is a group of similar vessels defined by vessel length group, main fishing technology and geo indicator, operating predominantly in one supra-region. The fs_name column encodes all of those. Where segments have been clustered, all members of the cluster share one fs_name and the name ends with an asterisk.

Confidentiality. Data for segments containing fewer than ten vessels may have been excluded altogether or merged into a cluster. This is the origin of the gaps that the processing routine fills with average prices.

Currency. All monetary values in this workbook are in nominal euro. The Annual Economic Report published from the same data uses 2023 constant prices instead. Any comparison we make across years needs its own deflation step, and we should state the base year explicitly.

Provisional data. Values for 2024, where available, may be provisional, incomplete or subject to revision.

Relationship to the published report. The workbook may not match the tables in the 2025 Annual Economic Report, which applied further checks and exclusions. Data coverage and quality issues are not flagged in the workbook itself.

Methodology and variable definitions are documented at stecf.jrc.ec.europa.eu/reports and datacollection.jrc.ec.europa.eu/data-calls.

The latest year that can be valued in full is 2023. The 2024 landings can be reported in physical terms with a clear provisional caveat, but they cannot carry a monetary valuation until the next data call fills in the expenditure side.

Regenerating the files

Download the workbook from the STECF data dissemination page, place it in data/stecf/ under its original name, and run the script from the project root. Nothing else in the repository needs to change; the processing routine reads the RDS files, not the workbook.
